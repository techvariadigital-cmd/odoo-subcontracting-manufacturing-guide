# Odoo Subcontracting: Managing Inventory and Costs in Outsourced Manufacturing

Outsourcing manufacturing operations gives businesses access to specialized production capabilities without requiring every process to take place in their own facilities. A company may send components to an external supplier for machining, finishing, assembly, or packaging, then receive the completed products back into inventory.
Although this arrangement offers flexibility, it introduces important operational questions. Where are company-owned materials while they are with a subcontractor? How much material should be consumed? What happens when actual production differs from expectations? And how can a manufacturer calculate the real cost of the finished product?

Odoo subcontracting workflows can help connect these activities, provided the system is configured to reflect the company's production process.

## Why Inventory Tracking Matters in Subcontracting?

When materials leave a manufacturer's warehouse, they do not necessarily leave the company's ownership. Depending on the agreement, components may remain company-owned while stored at an external supplier's facility.

For example, a manufacturer might send 500 metal components for machining. The supplier returns 470 finished parts, reports 15 damaged components, and confirms that 10 unused pieces remain on-site. Five components are still unaccounted for.
Without accurate records, the manufacturer may overestimate available stock, purchase unnecessary replacements, or plan production around materials that cannot be used immediately.

A reliable subcontracting process should track:

- Materials dispatched to each subcontractor.
- Stock held at external locations.
- Expected material consumption.
- Finished goods received.
- Scrap, rejected components, and unused materials.
- Differences between expected and actual quantities.

Regular reconciliation helps maintain a more accurate view of inventory across the production network.

## The Role of Bills of Materials

A Bill of Materials (BOM) defines the components and quantities expected to produce a finished product. In subcontracting, it can establish the materials required for the external operation.
Suppose an Automobile parts manufacturer outsources the assembly of a component that requires a housing, fasteners, seals, and connectors. The BOM provides a reference for the expected material quantities.

Actual consumption may differ because of scrap, rejected parts, or unused components. Manufacturers should compare expected requirements with actual production results and investigate significant differences.

Keeping BOMs current is essential. Outdated specifications can affect purchasing decisions, production planning, inventory accuracy, and Manufacturing costing.

## Connecting Purchase Orders With Inventory Movements

A Purchase Order records the commercial terms of the subcontracting arrangement, such as the finished product, quantity, and agreed processing charge. However, the order alone does not explain every movement of company-owned materials.

An appropriately configured Odoo workflow can connect subcontracting orders with Bills of Materials and inventory movements. The precise steps depend on the subcontracting model and system configuration.

A practical process includes:

1. Defining the finished product and its expected material requirements.
2. Creating the subcontracting Purchase Order.
3. Recording materials sent to the subcontractor.
4. Receiving completed goods into inventory.
5. Recording or reconciling unused materials and production losses.
6. Reviewing the costs associated with the finished products.

Connecting these records helps purchasing, warehouse, and production teams understand what was ordered, what materials were supplied, and what was ultimately received.

## Understanding the Full Cost of Outsourced Production

The subcontractor's processing fee is only one part of the cost assessment. Depending on the arrangement, manufacturers may also need to consider the cost of supplied materials, freight, inspection, rework, and production losses.
Manufacturing costing should follow the company's chosen costing method and applicable accounting policies.

Landed Cost may be useful for allocating eligible additional acquisition expenses to products when the transaction and system configuration support that treatment. Not every expense automatically qualifies, so businesses should review the relevant accounting requirements.

Comparing suppliers only by processing price can also be misleading. One supplier may charge less but produce more rejected units, while another may charge a higher fee and deliver more consistent output. Reviewing relevant costs alongside quality and delivery performance provides a more complete basis for supplier decisions.

## Managing Scrap and Material Differences

Not every production difference indicates a problem. Some processes naturally generate scrap, while others may occasionally produce rejected components or unexpected material consumption.

Manufacturers should distinguish expected process losses from unexplained discrepancies. When differences exceed reasonable expectations, teams can investigate whether the cause relates to material quality, production methods, equipment, outdated BOMs, or incomplete reporting.

Consistent records help identify recurring issues and support evidence-based discussions with subcontractors.

## Making Subcontracting Work More Reliably

Technology is only one part of an effective subcontracting process. Businesses also need clear responsibilities for dispatching materials, confirming receipts, reporting scrap, reconciling stock, and reviewing supplier invoices.
Odoo consultants may help evaluate whether the configured workflow reflects actual manufacturing requirements. Appropriate Odoo support & maintenance can help keep the system aligned as products, suppliers, and operating processes change.

Ultimately, successful subcontracting depends on maintaining visibility over materials, production output, and costs, even when the work takes place outside the company's own facilities.

By connecting inventory records, BOMs, Purchase Orders, and cost reviews, manufacturers can make more informed decisions about production planning, supplier performance, and future outsourcing arrangements.
